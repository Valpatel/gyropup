# Leg pre-print review (issue #29)

The owner, 2026-11-01:

> make sure you keep pushing the real gyropup design so we can begin 3d printing legs soon and testing them. Think of
> the things we will figure out are obvious issues -after- we print, and address them now instead. For example in high
> stress locations a bushing through plastic and threaded rods, nuts, etc can be better than thermal inserts. But
> thermal inserts are also great where possible.

This review goes through every printed leg part and every leg joint. For each failure mode, it asks what a first
print or a first load test would show, and it puts a number on everything the repository can compute. It sets the
joint policy that the owner's message asks for ([D74](../../docs/decisions.yaml)) and specifies four coupons to print
before any leg part.

- **The numbers** are in [screen.md](screen.md), generated by [screen.py](screen.py) from the sources it binds. When a
  bound line changes, the screen goes stale and the test fails.
- **The print-orientation screen** is [orientation_screen.py](orientation_screen.py). It ran on the compute host and
  its output is in [evidence/](evidence/).
- **Changes:** the review below was written against `dev` before any fix (PR #147), and its hand-written numbers are
  from then. [screen.md](screen.md) follows the current CAD, so on later commits it shows the fixes. What the second PR
  changed and what its FEA rerun found are in [docs/leg-print-package.md](../../docs/leg-print-package.md).

**What this is not:** CAD and FEA prove neither fit nor strength. The layer check is a linear, isotropic solve
resolved on the layer plane, and its interlayer shear allowable has not been measured. The press-fit and creep
numbers are screens built on stated assumptions. The coupons below, the B0–B6 bench tests and the proof loads in
issue #29 settle the questions.

## Failure modes, part by part

### Interlayer peel

The record's layer check (cad-agent v0.6.0, the version pinned in the leg records; v0.10.0 has the same material rows)
is tabulated in [screen.md](screen.md#layer-check-the-record). Every part prints with its layers stacked along its y,
which is the axis of every boss, bore and pin. Three kinds of geometry fail:

- **A boss standing out of a face along the build direction, loaded in bending.** Its root sees bending stress across
  the layers. This covers the upper link's knee-eye stub, the wheel link's apex hub and its knee boss (E1 1.44 against
  2.0).
- **A thin ring around a bearing, loaded by the bearing's couple.** This is the front link's apex ring.
- **The hip frame's flange, at its inner face** (R3o 0.93 against 1.0, E3o 1.89 against 2.0). The frame's E1 also
  misses on the gate (1.63 against 2.0). Issue #29 records six geometry trials; its proof load to 2 × E1 stands.

For the failing rows, the "x" column in screen.md gives the factor by which the peak stress must fall. For a root
loaded in bending, the radius must grow by the cube root of that factor. The "S_s needed" column gives the interlayer
shear strength that would let the row pass unchanged. Wherever σn is tensile, no plausible S_s is enough: σn alone
already exceeds the target's share of S_z, so the geometry has to change.

[orientation_screen.py](orientation_screen.py) resolved the same solves on two other layer planes, on edge (z) and on
end (x); see [screen.md](screen.md#print-orientation-layer-check-on-three-layer-planes). Every orientation's best case
still falls short:

- **Upper link, on edge:** R1 rises from 0.69 to 0.99, but R3K stays at 0.48 and R2 falls from 0.80 to 0.62.
- **Front link:** on edge it reaches only 0.55 (R1) and 0.80 (E1).
- **Wheel link, on edge:** R1w rises from 0.70 to 0.90.
- **Hip frame, on a flange face:** R3o (1.09) and E3o (2.23) pass, but E1 stays at 1.45. On end, the frame is
  274 mm tall, which is over the bed's 256.

Every orientation other than today's also turns the bearing seats, the sleeve bores and the dowel holes horizontal.
They then print out of round, and supports end up inside them. **Orientation is not the fix. Geometry, and metal
where geometry cannot reach, is.**

### Inserts: pull-out, spin-out, thin walls

The leg has only six heat-set inserts per leg, all in the hip frame's flange ends (`leg.hip_frame_mount`):

- **Pull-out:** about 1,500 N, estimated by the insert inventory from CNC Kitchen's M3 tests in PETG and scaled to
  humid PA6-CF across the layers. No PA6-CF insert has been pulled out.
- **Wall:** 2.94 mm each side, against Spirol's rule of a boss at least 2 × the insert's diameter. The flange is 1.97 ×.
- **Spin-out:** not measured.

A through-bolt removes all three (D74). C2 still pulls inserts in this exact wall, because the body and the turret
keep theirs.

### Crush, creep and preload loss

Plastic clamped under a head creeps and sheds preload. With the bolt about ten times stiffer than the plastic, every
0.05 mm the plastic gives costs most of that step × the plastic's stiffness. The losses are tabulated in
[screen.md](screen.md#clamped-plastic-and-preload). When humid, the modulus across the layers is 488 MPa, so the
squeeze is large: 1.8 mm under an M5 head on the hub today, at the assumed 1,200 N. A sleeved joint loses nothing to
the plastic's creep. A clamp ring loses about 7 % per 0.05 mm, so the hub joint gets a re-torque at 24 h and after the
first humid week, and C3 measures the real creep.

### Pin bearing in printed eyes

R3 puts 37.9 MPa on the knee eye (allowable 40, SF 1.06), spread along the two 20 mm sleeves. That is the undamped
power cut. With the rig's bumper, or the measured RS-04 damping, the knee load falls 5.6 × (2,048 N to 364 N).
Everywhere else in the leg, the pins bear on steel sleeves or bearings, never on plastic.

### Bearing seats: creep, retention, shrinkage

The press fits are tabulated in [screen.md](screen.md#press-and-slide-fits-in-printed-holes):

- **The 6301 and 61812 seats** are drawn 0.04–0.05 mm loose, so once printed they land between line-to-line and a
  0.16 mm press. That is fine, and C1 sets it.
- **Retention:** every seat stops on a web, so each axial load direction reaches one bearing's shoulder. The
  knees and the apex already use this X arrangement. The press fit only has to stop the outer race from creeping
  round.
- **Inspection:** a paint witness line across each race and seat shows race creep at each inspection.
- **Replaceability:** D39 replaces the knee bearings after an undamped cut, which rules out gluing them.

### Elephant's foot

Every press mouth that opens on the bed face gets squeezed by the first layers:

- the knee-eye sleeves (pressed in from the bed face);
- one 6301 seat in each lower link;
- the inner 61812 seat;
- the stop-pin holes.

PR 2 chamfers each of these 0.5 × 45°, and C1 prints half of its mouths plain to show the difference. Keep the
slicer's elephant-foot compensation on as well.

### Supports, scars, warping

- **Hip frame:** 35 cm² of overhang, mostly 6–8 mm up.
- **Wheel link:** 20 cm², mostly at 5 mm.
- **Front link:** floats (finding 1).
- **Bed:** PA6-CF on a 100 °C bed in the enclosed P1S warps less than unfilled PA. The hip frame (274 × 158, turned
  45°) and the two lower links (about 320 long, turned) are the parts whose corners lift. Use a 5 mm brim and
  measure each bed face's flatness after printing (sheet in the print package, PR 2).
- **Scars:** support scars must not land on a bearing face or a stop face. With the collar split, nothing in the leg
  prints on supports under a working face.

### Moisture and drying

- **Recipe:** 80 °C for 8–12 h, then a dry feed below 20 % RH
  (Bambu's PA6-CF guidance, as [docs/rs04-fit-job.md](../../docs/rs04-fit-job.md) records it).
- **Long prints:** each leg part runs 26–73 h, so the feed must stay dry for the whole print.
- **Spools:** the hip frame needs a second spool.
- **In service:** the parts take up water. That is the humid design basis (strength × 0.5, modulus × 0.29), and it
  takes most of the press fits' grip.
- **Measuring it:** C1 weighs its blocks dry and after a humid week, so the swelling and the grip loss are measured,
  not assumed.

### Motor-to-leg torque transfer

- **Hip frame to motor:** the motor's torque goes into the hip frame through two Ø5 dowels (1,132 N each at 19 MPa).
  The lug screws hold the motor on, but the plastic under their heads limits them to 565 N each. A crush sleeve under
  each M4 head (D74) moves that limit to the motor's thread. It is held for the RS-04 support work (issue #116), whose
  fit job prints this ring.
- **Motor to upper link:** the torque reaches the upper link through the machined adapter (D46): two dowels and the
  hub clamp (finding 7).

### Shaft and pin retention; fasteners backing out

- **Knee pin:** an ISO 7379 shoulder screw, clamped by an M10 nyloc to 20 N·m on steel (two sleeves, a steel spacer,
  inner races), so it cannot back out while the stack is steel.
- **The knee spacer:** today it is a 5/8 in 6061 tube, under the 6301's abutment (da min 17.6 mm). PR 2 replaces it
  with a steel spacer of 18–19.4 mm.
- **Apex:** a DIN 471-60 circlip in a printed groove, with the axial load only the wheel's side grip.
- **Screws into plastic-clamped joints:** these lose preload to creep and are the ones that back out. Hence the
  re-torque schedule and the sleeves.

### Five-bar tolerance stack and backlash

With the motors held, 0.1 mm of radial play at any joint moves the axle 0.10–0.14 mm, and 0.1° at a motor moves it
0.18–0.26 mm (crouch, stand and tall). The plays add up to:

- **knees:** a few hundredths, from the shoulder screw in its F7 sleeves and the 6301's clearance;
- **apex:** up to 0.1 mm, from the 61812's inner races on the printed Ø60 journal (B0's hub-stub coupon sets this).

So the axle wanders about ±0.2 mm, small next to a tyre's give. The motors' backlash, measured on B1, will dominate.

### Assembly order and tool access

In this order:

1. Motors onto the frame (dowels first, then the lugs).
2. The adapter onto each motor.
3. The upper links (hub ring and screws, from the outer face).
4. Knees (the sleeves pressed from the outer face, the 6301s pressed into each lower link from both ends; then the
   shoulder screw from the lower link's outer side and the nut in the eye's pocket).
5. Apex (the 61812s pressed into the front link from both sides, the spacer, the wheel link's hub through, then the
   circlip).
6. The wheel last, its 4 × M6 from inside the hub through the Ø48 bore.

With through-bolts, the hip-frame mount's nuts go into hex pockets on the web's leg face. This must happen before the
frame meets the body: the screws then come from the cheek side, as today. PR 2's print package carries the full
sequence and the torque table.

### Cable routing

The wheel's leads (9 mm sleeved) pass through the Ø48 hub bore and the Ø22 foot hole, round the rear knee and along
the rear upper link. They flex at every knee motion, so the cable must be a high-flex type, tied with strain relief
at the foot and at the hip, with a service loop at the knee. Chafe on the Ø22 hole's bed-face edge is another reason
to chamfer it.

### Bed size and print time

Every part fits the 256 mm bed. The two lower links (318–320 mm) and the hip frame (274 mm) fit only when turned
diagonally. A leg is 204 h and 3.08 kg of PA6-CF.

### Falls and the stops

The undamped power cut (`power_off`) sets every R3 margin:

- **Loads:** 2,048 N on a knee and 122 N·m on a stop.
- **Damped:** with the rig's bumper or the RS-04's power-off damping (`power_off_damped`), these drop to 364 N and
  15 N·m.
- **Drops:** a 150 mm drop puts 1,605 N on the axle and 975 N on the knee.
- **Proof:** the rig test with the bumper set, and the bearing inspection after any undamped cut, stay as issue #29
  lists them.

## Print-first coupons

Print these before any leg part, in the leg's recipe and on the same printer. Write the results into the sheets
next to them.

| Coupon | What it settles | Sheet |

| **C2** insert pull-out in the real 2.94 mm wall (B4a) | pull-out and spin-out across the layers, in place of the 1,500 N estimate | [c2-insert.csv](sheets/c2-insert.csv) |

| **C4** boss-root layer coupon (the knee stub), today's geometry and PR 2's | the load at which the root peels, against the FEA's prediction for the same coupon. It calibrates S_z and the unmeasured S_s | [c4-boss-root.csv](sheets/c4-boss-root.csv) |

The B0 coupons need updating along with these. The `coupon_knee_boss` docstring and the bench page still describe
the 6201, and its seat is 10 mm deep for the 12 mm-wide 6301. B4c (the M8 insert for the stop posts) is moot since
D53. PR 2 draws C1–C4 in `cad/rig_bench.py` and packages them.

**To print and test these, the owner needs:**

- PA6-CF;
- a few DIN 179 18 × 12 × 20 sleeves, 6301-2RS and 61812 bearings, 5 mm m6 dowels and Ø16 stop pins (the leg's
  own parts);
- M5 8.8 screws and nuts;
- 8 × 5.3 mm tube (6061 or steel) for the C3 sleeves;
- a structural epoxy for the stop pins (and, to try on C1, LOCTITE 7649 activator with the 638).

The press cage, the load cell and the pullers are B3 and B4's. No purchases have been made.

## Owner decisions open

1. **The apex: printed or metal.**
   - The wheel link's hub root needs its peel stress × 0.58 (0.70 against 1.2) and the front link's ring × 0.43.
   - A turned 6061 hub costs about +120 g a leg, and also puts the wheel's M6 heads on metal.
   - A printed root fillet and a thicker ring are lighter, but nothing yet shows they reach the target.
   - PR 2 sweeps both and reports the masses.
2. **The hip-frame mount's sleeve: 6061 or steel.** Steel is +67 g a leg more and costs about the same. 6061 at
   5.1 kN is at 181 MPa, so it relies on the plastic around it to keep it straight.
3. **Mass.** The D74 changes above add roughly 150–300 g a leg, depending on the apex choice. D71's as-built gate is
   37.0 kg, and the robot's ledger has been reported at 38.55 kg. D72 raised the product ceiling to 45.36 kg but asks
   for the affected load cases at the real mass before manufacturing.
