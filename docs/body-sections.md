## 1. Skid and power

Carries the battery and turns it into every rail the robot needs: the 10S bus for the legs, the wheels and the turret, 12 V for the J501 and the sensors, 5 V for the MCU; protects every wire with a fuse sized to it; lets the packs out without tools.

**Must meet:**

- two DeWalt DCB609 packs in series, 30-41 V, 360 Wh rated (D65); swapped by hand, no tool
- every load rated over 30-41 V: the RS-04 24-60 V, the Flipsky 75100 14-84 V, the anti-spark switch 12-84 V, the TRACO TEP 150-4812WI 18-75 V (the DDR-120C-24 starts at 33.6 V and is out)
- 80 A for 0.5 s (a jump's push) and 30 A continuous (the adapters' stated rating): the firmware limits both
- the bay under the belly between the knees: |y| <= 58 (25 mm from the knee eyes at |y| 83)
- the robot's only battery protection is its own: cut-off at 3.0 V a cell, a current limit, a regen clamp (docs/body-firmware.md 5)

**Interfaces:**

- the belly plate: the bay's rails and adapter plates on S-M4-0 nuts beyond the cheeks' ends (|x| 102, 150)
- the floor tray: the power plate's four printed posts (M5 inserts both ends); the tray slot (x 40..135) for the cables
- the legs: four XT60H-F sockets for the RS-04 pigtails; the wheels' VESCs under the plate
- the J501: 12 V on an XT30 (W21); CAN bus C (the MCU)
- the turret (D66): its XT30 on a 7.5 A branch of the bus through the stop relay's pole B; its dry safety loop

## 6. The mass gate (D71)

The P3 ledger is 38.56 kg; with every scheduled lever 38.06 kg against D71's 37.0 kg ceiling (37.5 kg design mass, 18.75 kg a leg): a margin of -1.06 kg, OVER. The wheels are D69's 10 in hub motors at the listed 3.53 kg each; the battery group is 3.27 kg (two DCB609 at DeWalt US's 1565 g each). The head is still the 1.70 kg budget: the turret as its own robot (D66) weighs 2.55 kg on its branch, which uses about 0.85 kg of the margin when it lands.
