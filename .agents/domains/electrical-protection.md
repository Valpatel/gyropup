# Electrical protection limits and evidence

Separate documented steady supply voltage from a measured fault/transient envelope. A TVS standoff voltage, minimum breakdown, maximum clamp at a specified current, and pulse power rating describe different conditions. Check the maximum breakdown and clamping voltage against every protected device; do not use the minimum breakdown as a guaranteed terminal ceiling. Source impedance, wiring inductance, pulse duration/repetition and temperature determine the actual waveform and energy.

Active protection also needs tolerances, hysteresis, overdrive-dependent response, external gate load, MOSFET safe operating area and board thermal evidence. A nominal cutoff below a device limit is insufficient. Trace whether a stage protects one converter or a shared branch: current/inrush, downstream limits and fault behavior differ. Firmware torque limits and fuse names are not measured current bounds.

The [carrier protection comparison](../../research/protection/carrier-input-protection.md) and source-bound calculator retain unqualified dynamic peaks as null, not as assumed passes. Static source/voltage/load arithmetic does not establish an enclosure temperature, output-regeneration limit or physical safety. Candidate package dimensions are not a completed PCB footprint; electrical selection must precede final carrier fit/print release. Preserve the current supplier/revision rules in [supplier evidence](supplier-evidence.md).

For generated editable schematics, validate **the EDA tool's exported netlist**, not only the source's intended connections. Hidden power pins can create implicit global supply nets; keep all physical duplicate leads in the contract and run all-severity ERC. Preserve separate manufacturer and project-assigned pad numbering, top/bottom orientation, converter VIN versus bypass input, and distinct EN/PG connections. The [native circuit candidate](../../hardware/carrier-pan-protection/README.md) includes actual KiCad corruption/export tests and the [hidden-power-pin incident](../inbox/2026-10-29-hidden-power-pins-can-create-unintended-global-nets.md). A clean ERC is connectivity evidence, not a circuit qualification or arm interlock.

A reverse-input headline is insufficient when output capacitance is charged: calculate every relevant pin-to-pin differential and include hot-insertion coupling. Trace whether an existing diode is series or shunt before crediting it; a shunt plus a nominal fuse is not established I²t coordination. Startup requires output load converted to input current at worst enabled voltage/efficiency, capacitance charging, comparator startup thresholds and enable sequencing. Preserve failed corners and conditional assumptions as explicit qualification gates. The [charged-reversal/startup incident](../inbox/2026-10-29-charged-output-changes-reverse-input-stress.md) records real values and regressions. Distributor “equivalent” variants must be checked against the manufacturer's function table; UV and OV supervisors are not interchangeable.

Timing bounds are conditional on the programmed delay pins and specified test setup. A capacitor on a supervisor's CTR pin can add to startup time as well as release delay. Bind those pin/net conditions in the calculator and independent native contract; retain actual pullup, output loading and slew differences as qualification gates. The charged-reversal incident records the review-caught TPS3760 correction and restored-capacitor negative control.

An extended-range application circuit is the complete topology, not the controller's headline rating. Preserve floating references, sense resistors and clamps, then validate each pin's waveform and pulse/temperature limits. A low-resistance path's enhanced-state margin is not its initial body-diode/startup margin. Thermal characterization copper/board dimensions are examples, not allocated carrier space or a demonstrated minimum footprint. Coordinate actual contour, heights and cable/thermal zones before fitting a circuit. See the [reference-circuit and thermal-area incident](../inbox/2026-10-29-reference-circuit-thermal-area-is-not-carrier-fit.md) and its source-bound architecture tests.
